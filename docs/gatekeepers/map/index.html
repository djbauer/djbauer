Your turn

Prototype for the Gatekeepers design draft. The other houses are simple computer players. In the real game they would be your friends, and everyone's orders would resolve together once a day. Battles use exactly the same rules as the battle prototype, and the round report plays them back.

How to play

Lead your house to the most prestige while the jump gates fail around you. Each round you work through five steps, then everyone's orders resolve together.

Winning

  • Most prestige after the last round wins.
  • Ascendancy: from the halfway round, a house with 25% more prestige than every rival wins at once.
  • If 5 gates go dark, the empire collapses and everyone loses.
  • Prestige comes from holding systems, winning battles, taking systems, repairing gates and your secret objective.

1 · Council

  • Vote yes or no on one decree. Spend influence for extra votes.
  • Table a motion: the one with the most influence behind it is next round's decree.
  • Secretly send fuel to the failing gate (amber). Too little and it goes dark for good.

2 · Fleets

  • You get 3 orders: move a fleet one step, or build ships at a system you own.
  • Three parts: Move, Build, then Formation (free).
  • Rival fleets in the same system fight automatically, using the formation and doctrine you set.
  • Be the only fleet in a system at round's end to take it. Owned systems need 2+ squadrons (militia).

3 · Diplomacy

  • Press Talk to make treaties from clauses: peace, trade, shared sight, repairs, votes, tribute.
  • Your envoy leaves when you end the round. The answer (yes, no or a counter-offer) is in the round report.
  • Treaties are public and enforced. Keeping them earns honour; betraying one costs dearly.
  • Answer envoys before the round ends, or their houses take it as a snub.

4 · At home

  • Mandate (0 to 10) is how firmly your people back you.
  • Victories, light taxes and festivals raise it; defeats, heavy taxes and unjust wars lower it.
  • Popular houses gain influence; a house in revolt loses an order and its fresh conquests.

5 · Review

  • Check every choice, go back to change any of them, then confirm.
  • The round report shows what happened and plays back your battles.
More rules
  • Resources versus standing: credits, fuel and influence are spent. Prestige is your score; honour and mandate each add or take 2 to 3 prestige per point away from 5 at the end.
  • Claims: your people back a war against a house that took your system, fought you at home, raided you, betrayed you or defied your ultimatum (8 rounds), or that the council censured, embargoed, sanctioned or crusaded against (3 rounds). Taking a system without a claim costs 1 mandate. Conquered systems pay nothing for 3 rounds.
  • Seven houses: each is stronger at some of fleets, diplomacy and the home front, with its own ship and gambit (see Your house, under the map).
  • Leaving a treaty: denounce it (ends after this round, no penalty) or betray it (ends before tonight's battles; −3 honour, −10 prestige and a sanction motion against you).
  • Gate tolls: crossing a gate costs fuel, paid to whoever holds the far side. Trade partners cross free.
  • Fog of war: you only see fleets in and next to your systems and fleets.
  • Nobody is knocked out: a house with no systems can build at the Concord capital.
  • Missing a round: standing orders cover the first missed round; from the second, a Concord steward runs your house (settings in "While you're away", under the map).
  • Income: the top bar shows what each resource will gain next round; hover over it, or see Your house, for where it comes from. Fuel only comes from fuel worlds (blue) and tolls paid to you.
  • The map: scroll or use + and − to zoom, drag to move around, Fit to see everything.
