Your house

Prototype for the Gatekeepers design draft. The other houses are simple computer players. In the real game they would be your friends, and everyone's orders would resolve together once a day. Battles use exactly the same rules as the battle prototype, and each one can be replayed from the round report.

How to play

  • Goal: have the most prestige when the last round ends. Prestige comes from holding systems (1 per system each round), winning battles, taking systems, repairing gates and your secret objective.
  • Shared threat: each round one jump gate is failing. Everyone can secretly send fuel to repair it. If too little arrives, it goes dark for good and the routes through it close. If 5 gates go dark, the empire collapses and everyone loses.
  • Three orders a round: move a fleet one step, build ships at a system you own, send resources to another house, or govern at home (a festival or a proclamation). Voting, taxes, gate repairs, diplomacy and fleet settings are free.
  • Moving: select your fleet, press Move, then click a neighbouring system. Crossing a jump gate costs fuel.
  • Battles: when rival fleets end up in the same system, they fight automatically using their formation, doctrine and retreat setting. The Concord capital is neutral ground: no battles there.
  • Taking systems: a system is yours when your fleet is the only one there at the end of a round. Taking a system another house owns needs at least 2 squadrons (its militia fights off smaller raids): 3 if its owner is popular, just 1 if its owner is in revolt. A conquered system is restless for 3 rounds and yields nothing yet.
  • The home front: every house has a public mandate out of 10: how firmly its people back it. Victories, light taxes, festivals and proclamations raise it. Defeats, lost systems, heavy taxes, dark gates beside your systems, council decrees against you, paying tribute and wars without a claim lower it. Popular houses (7+) get more influence and tougher militia. Restless houses (3 or less) suffer strikes. A house in revolt (1 or less) has only 2 orders, its militia folds and its fresh conquests break away.
  • Claims: your people back a war against a house that has taken your system, fought you in your own system, raided you, betrayed you or defied your ultimatum (for 8 rounds), or that the council has censured, embargoed, sanctioned or crusaded against (3 rounds). Taking a system from a house you have no claim on costs you 1 mandate, and its people rally instead of losing heart.
  • Seven houses: each is stronger at some of fleets, diplomacy and the home front, with its own ship and its own gambit (Varkas ultimatums, Solenne's gate writs, Meridian loans, Kestrel smugglers, Nyx whisper campaigns, Ashgrove aid, Synod crusades). See Your house.
  • Council: each round there is one decree. Vote yes or no, and spend influence for extra votes. You can also table a motion: the motion with the most influence behind it becomes next round's decree.
  • Diplomacy: press Talk next to a house to negotiate. Treaties bundle clauses (non-aggression, trade, shared sight, a repair compact, council backing, tribute) for 3, 5 or 10 rounds. The Concord seals them for 1 influence each and enforces them, and everyone can see them.
  • Honour: every house has a public honour score out of 10 (your reputation abroad, as mandate is your standing at home). Binding treaties of 5 or more rounds that run their full term earn +1. Falling short on a payment costs 1. At the end, each point above 5 is worth +3 prestige, and each point below 5 costs 3.
  • Leaving a treaty: denounce it (it ends after this round, no penalty) or betray it (it ends before tonight's battles, but you lose 3 honour and 10 prestige, your victim earns double prestige against you for 5 rounds, and the Concord tables a sanction against you).
  • Gate tolls: crossing a gate costs fuel, paid to whoever holds the system on the far side. Trade partners cross each other's gates free.
  • Fog of war: you only see fleets in and next to your systems and fleets.
  • Nobody is knocked out: a house down to one system gets extra credits to rebuild, and a house with no systems can commission ships at the Concord capital.
  • Missing a round: if you don't send orders, your standing orders apply: fleets hold, treaty payments continue, your standing gate repair goes out, and you vote with the majority. From the second missed round, a Concord steward runs your house with the settings in "While you're away". Stewards keep every treaty, never betray and start no wars. You're back in charge the moment you send orders again.
